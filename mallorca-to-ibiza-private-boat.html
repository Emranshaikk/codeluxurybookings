How Long Does a Private Boat Take from Mallorca to Ibiza?

The open-water distance across the Balearic channel from Mallorca to Ibiza is approximately 150 kilometres (93 statute miles / 81 nautical miles) between port centers. However, there is no universal single crossing time. The duration of your voyage varies depending on several operational and logistical factors:

  • Departure Marina: Departing from western Mallorca (Port d'Andratx) cuts the sea distance down to ~50–55 nautical miles, saving 30 to 45 minutes of transit compared to central Palma (~70 nautical miles).
  • Arrival Marina: Docking in northern Ibiza (Santa Eulària / Portinatx) or eastern Ibiza (Marina Botafoch / Marina Ibiza) takes less time than circumnavigating to western harbors like San Antonio.
  • Boat Type & Cruising Speed: High-performance planning hulls (such as Wajer or Vanquish cruisers) cruise at 30 to 38 knots, completing the run in ~2.5 hours. Heavier displacement motor yachts typically cruise at 20 to 24 knots (~3.5 hours), while sailing catamarans average 8 to 12 knots (~5 to 7 hours).
  • Sea & Weather Conditions: Early morning crossings benefit from calm, glassy seas. Mid-afternoon thermal winds (the Balearic embat) can build light chop that may adjust cruising speed for passenger comfort.
  • Stops & Itinerary: Adding an offshore swim at Espalmador or an extended Mediterranean lunch on Formentera expands the voyage into a full day experience.

Where Can a Private Boat Arrive in Ibiza?

Arrival marinas in Ibiza are subject to berth availability, vessel draft, charter agreement terms, and local maritime authority permissions. Below are the primary entry gateways:

  • Marina Botafoch: Located on the north side of the Port of Ibiza directly opposite the UNESCO-listed Dalt Vila. The top choice for motor yachts, day cruisers, and speedboats, offering immediate walking access to renowned evening venues like Lío, IT Ibiza, and Roto Club.
  • Marina Ibiza: One of the Mediterranean's most exclusive superyacht marinas, accommodating mega yachts up to 100+ metres. Features five-star VIP concierge facilities, high-end fashion boutiques (Loewe, Dior, Louis Vuitton), and the famous Lío cabaret restaurant.
  • Ibiza Magna / Old Port: Nestled directly beneath the historic medieval castle walls. Exceptionally scenic and prestigious, though berth allocation is strictly limited and reserved primarily for large sailing and motor yachts.
  • San Antonio Marina (Bahía de Sant Antoni): Positioned on Ibiza's western coastline. Highly practical if your accommodation is in western Ibiza (Cala Conta, Cala Tarida) or if you plan to catch the legendary sunset off Café del Mar.
  • Approved Anchorage Drop-Offs: Weather permitting, tenders can transfer guests ashore to private beach clubs or coves such as Cala Jondal (Blue Marlin / Casa Jondal), Talamanca, or Tagomago island.

Private Boat vs Ferry from Mallorca to Ibiza

Commercial ferry operators (Balearia and Trasmed) run scheduled ferry services between Palma and Ibiza. While ferries provide essential public transport, a private yacht charter delivers a fundamentally different luxury experience:

Feature Private Boat Charter Commercial Ferry
Privacy 100% Private — your group and crew only Shared with 400 to 900+ passengers
Departure Time Flexible — depart at your chosen hour Fixed commercial timetable
Route & Itinerary Customizable — direct or via Formentera/coves Fixed commercial shipping corridor
Swimming Stops Yes — anchor in crystal-clear coves No stops permitted
Onboard Catering Bespoke fine dining, champagne, private chef options Standard commercial cafeteria options
Luggage Handling Flexible luggage storage handled by crew Subject to operator baggage rules & queues
Best For Families, private groups, celebrations, luxury travellers Solo travellers & budget-conscious passengers

Honest Assessment: Private boat charters are not designed to compete with ferry ticket prices on a budget basis. For individual solo passengers or travellers whose primary consideration is low cost, public commercial ferries remain the practical economic option. Private charters cater to travellers seeking autonomy, privacy, bespoke service, and memorable experiences.

Is a Mallorca to Ibiza Private Boat Worth It?

A private charter between the islands represents a significant investment. Understanding who benefits most helps clarify whether it is the right choice for your holiday:

Who Benefits Most:

  • Families with Children: Complete freedom to move, safe swim stops, personalized meal timings, and zero commercial terminal stress.
  • Private Groups & Celebrations: Bachelor/bachelorette trips, birthdays, or milestone anniversaries where celebrating together on the water is part of the experience.
  • Couples Seeking Seclusion: Romantic open-sea crossings with sunset champagne and private beach anchorages away from tourist crowds.
  • HNW & Executive Travellers: Time efficiency, discretion, confidentiality, and seamless coordination with private villa check-ins.
  • Travellers Wanting Formentera: Experiencing Formentera’s world-class waters without needing to book a separate day trip later in your holiday.

When the Ferry Makes More Sense:

  • Solo Travellers: Chartering a private boat alone carries high per-person costs compared to a single ferry ticket.
  • Strict Transportation-Only Focus: If the only goal is moving between hotels as cheaply as possible with no interest in sightseeing or swimming.
  • Travellers Comfortable with Fixed Schedules: If rigid departure times and waiting in commercial passenger terminals do not disrupt your schedule.

What's Included in a Private Charter?

Charter agreements in the Balearic Islands operate under clear maritime standards. While exact inclusions vary depending on the vessel category and charter agreement, here is what is typically included versus potential additional costs:

Standard Inclusions:

  • Professional licensed Spanish captain (skipper)
  • Deckhand / stewardess service (on larger yachts)
  • Standard onboard safety and navigation equipment
  • Complimentary mineral water, soft drinks, and ice
  • Fresh bath towels and sunbed linens
  • Snorkeling equipment and basic water toys (paddleboards)
  • Home port marina mooring fees (Mallorca)
  • Comprehensive marine passenger insurance

Potential Additional Costs:

  • Fuel: Calculated by engine hours and speed, or managed via APA (Advanced Provisioning Allowance).
  • Spanish VAT (IVA): 21% legal tax applied to commercial yacht charters in Spain.
  • Overnight Marina Mooring: If docking overnight in prime Ibiza marinas (e.g. Marina Botafoch).
  • Specialized Provisioning: Fine wines, champagnes, gourmet catering, or private chef service.
  • Motorized Water Toys: Seabobs, e-foils, or jet skis (where permitted by local licenses).
  • Private Chauffeur Transfers: Hotel or airport ground transportation to and from the marina.

*Depending on the vessel and charter agreement, some day cruisers offer all-inclusive fuel packages for fixed routes. All anticipated costs are transparently detailed before charter contract signature.

Best Time to Travel from Mallorca to Ibiza

The Balearic charter season runs from May through October, each window offering distinct advantages for sea crossings:

  • May to June (Shoulder Season): Mild temperatures (22–27°C), uncrowded marinas, calm morning waters, and club opening parties. Charter pricing is typically 20% to 30% below midsummer peaks, making this a favorite for seasoned travellers.
  • July to August (Peak Season): The vibrant heart of the Mediterranean summer. Warm sea temperatures (26–28°C), lively beach clubs in Formentera and Ibiza, and long sunny daylight hours. Early booking is essential as top vessels reserve weeks in advance.
  • September to October (Late Season): Beautiful warm waters, golden light, gentle breezes, and relaxed anchorages as summer crowds depart. Sea conditions remain generally stable through early October.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a private boat from Mallorca to Ibiza cost?

Indicative day charter pricing starts from €8,000 for private day cruisers and speedboats (such as a Wajer 55 or Fjord 44), €8,500 to €14,000 for luxury catamarans, €10,500 to €18,000 for motor yachts, and €25,000+ per day for crewed superyachts. Exact charter rates depend on the season, vessel model, duration, fuel consumption, and requested onboard provisioning.

How long does a private boat take from Mallorca to Ibiza?

The open-water crossing distance is approximately 150 km (93 nautical miles). High-speed day cruisers and performance motor yachts typically complete the crossing in 2.5 to 3.5 hours from Port d'Andratx or Port Adriano. Luxury motor yachts take between 3.5 and 4.5 hours, while sailing and power catamarans average 4 to 7 hours depending on cruising speed, weather, and sea state.

What is the cheapest way to charter a private boat to Ibiza?

The most economical way to charter privately is booking during the shoulder months of May, June, or September/October when rates are 20% to 35% lower than peak July and August. Choosing a departure from Port d'Andratx also minimizes total nautical miles and fuel consumption compared to departing from central Palma.

Can I book a private boat from Palma to Ibiza?

Yes. Palma is Mallorca’s largest yachting hub with departures readily available from Club de Mar, Moll Vell, and Marina Port de Mallorca. The crossing from Palma is approximately 68 to 72 nautical miles, taking roughly 3 to 4.5 hours depending on vessel cruising speed.

Can I travel from Mallorca to Ibiza via Formentera?

Yes, including Formentera is one of the most requested itineraries for private charters. You can depart Mallorca in the morning, anchor off Espalmador or Ses Illetes for swimming and lunch at beach clubs like Beso Beach or Juan y Andrea, and cruise across to Ibiza by sunset.

Can I book a same-day return?

Yes, same-day return trips are possible with high-speed day cruisers and performance motor yachts capable of cruising at 30+ knots. An early departure (around 8:30–9:00 AM) from Port d'Andratx allows for 4 to 6 hours in Ibiza or Formentera before returning to Mallorca before dusk.

How many people can a private boat to Ibiza accommodate?

Under Spanish maritime charter regulations, most private day charter boats are commercially licensed for up to 10 or 12 guests plus crew. For larger parties or corporate groups, tandem charters (two yachts cruising together) or specialized event vessels can be coordinated.

Does the private boat price include fuel?

Day charter quotes typically exclude fuel unless explicitly stated as an all-inclusive package. Fuel is calculated based on engine hours and cruising speed, or handled via an Advanced Provisioning Allowance (APA) for larger yachts. On a return crossing, fuel typically ranges from €1,200 to €4,500 depending on vessel size and cruising speed.

Do I need to book a captain?

Yes. Open-water crossings between Mallorca and Ibiza require an experienced professional, commercially licensed skipper and deck crew who know Balearic maritime regulations, weather patterns, and local marina protocols. Bareboat charters for this crossing are rarely permitted by yacht underwriters due to the open-sea distance.

Can I bring luggage?

Yes. For one-way relocation charters between Mallorca and Ibiza, motor yachts and catamarans offer generous stateroom and hull storage for luggage. High-speed day cruisers have more compact storage, so soft-sided bags are recommended. Advise your concierge of your luggage volume in advance to ensure appropriate vessel matching.

What happens if the weather is bad?

Passenger safety and comfort are paramount. In the event of adverse sea conditions (such as high swell or gale-force winds) that make the 150 km crossing uncomfortable or unsafe, your captain will review alternative sheltered coastal cruising options or reschedule the crossing date subject to charter agreement terms.
