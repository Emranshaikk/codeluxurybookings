The Monaco to Amalfi Route Overview

The voyage from Port Hercule in Monaco to the sheer volcanic cliffs of the Amalfi Coast is the quintessential Mediterranean superyacht journey. Spanning approximately 450 nautical miles of cruising, this route traverses the most iconic maritime playgrounds in Western Europe. It seamlessly blends the high-stakes glamour of the French Riviera, the untouched, rugged citadel ports of Corsica, the turquoise swimming sanctuaries of Sardinia's La Maddalena, and the historic charm of Ponza, Capri, and Positano. Cruising this open-sea corridor requires a stable, high-performance yacht to ensure uncompromised guest comfort across the Ligurian and Tyrrhenian passages.

The 7-Day Day-by-Day Yacht Itinerary

Day 1: Monaco to Saint-Tropez

Embarkation in Port Hercule & Passage to the Riviera's Capital

Embark on your luxury superyacht in Monaco's prestigious Port Hercule. After a champagne welcome from your Captain and crew, cast off and cruise past the iconic cliffs of Eze and the red rocks of the Esterel coastline. Your destination is Saint-Tropez. Anchor in the turquoise waters of Pampelonne Beach for a late afternoon swim and tender ashore for dinner in the historic Vieux Port. Experience the vibrant nightlife of the town or dine quietly under the pine trees at the legendary Club 55.

Day 2: Saint-Tropez to Cannes & Lérins Islands

Cruising the Esterel Coast & Anchoring Between the Islands

Awake to a fresh breakfast prepared by your private chef as the yacht makes a short morning cruise to the Lérins Islands, situated just off the coast of Cannes. Anchor in the narrow, shallow channel between Île Sainte-Marguerite and Île Saint-Honorat. The crystal-clear, calm waters are ideal for deploying your yacht's tender, paddleboards, and jet skis. Spend the afternoon exploring the ancient fort where the "Man in the Iron Mask" was held, or visit the fortified monastery on Saint-Honorat where local monks have produced fine wine for centuries. Spend the evening docked on the Croisette in Cannes.

Day 3: Cannes to Calvi, Corsica

Passage Across the Ligurian Sea to the Genoese Citadel

Depart Cannes at dawn for an open-sea crossing of the Ligurian Sea (approx. 95 nautical miles). Relax in the jacuzzi on the sun deck as your stabilizers maintain absolute comfort. By afternoon, the dramatic mountains of Corsica will rise from the horizon, culminating in the historic harbor of Calvi. Drop anchor under the shadow of the massive 13th-century Genoese citadel. Tender to the marina and stroll through the narrow cobblestone streets, discovering local Corsican charcuterie, fine wines, and historic ramparts that boast panoramic sea views.

Day 4: Calvi to Bonifacio, Corsica

Cruising the Scandola Nature Reserve & Navigating the Fjord

Cruise south along Corsica's rugged western coast. You will pass the Scandola Nature Reserve—a UNESCO World Heritage site featuring dramatic red granite cliffs and volcanic caves rising from deep blue waters. After lunch on the deck, navigate into the spectacular limestone fjord of Bonifacio. Bonifacio's medieval town is perched precariously on 70-meter-high chalk cliffs. Navigating this narrow, deep harbor entrance is one of the most breathtaking maneuvers in yachting. Explore the clifftop fortress and enjoy dinner overlooking the marina.

Day 5: Bonifacio to La Maddalena & Porto Cervo, Sardinia

Turquoise Sanctuaries & High-Society Yachting

Cross the Strait of Bonifacio to Sardinia. Stop first in the Maddalena Archipelago, a national park featuring some of the clearest, most turquoise waters in the Mediterranean. Anchor off Budelli Island to view the famous pink sand beach (Spiaggia Rosa) and swim in the natural pools of Porto della Madonna. In the late afternoon, cruise to Porto Cervo, the crown jewel of Sardinia's Costa Smeralda. Dock at the Yacht Club Costa Smeralda and explore the designer boutiques, fine restaurants, and exclusive clubs that cater to the global elite.

Day 6: Porto Cervo to Capri, Italy

Tyrrhenian Sea Crossing & the Iconic Faraglioni Rocks

Depart Sardinia for a passage across the Tyrrhenian Sea to the island of Capri. Arriving in Capri, your captain will anchor near the legendary Faraglioni rocks, the limestone sea stacks that define the island's coastline. Take the tender to visit the Blue Grotto or swim in the emerald waters of the Green Grotto. As evening approaches, tender ashore to Marina Grande and take the funicular up to the famous Piazzetta. Enjoy an aperitivo before dining at a clifftop restaurant overlooking the Gulf of Naples.

Day 7: Capri to Positano & Amalfi Coast

Dramatic Amalfi Cliffs, Vertical Towns, and Disembarkation

Your final morning brings you to the spectacular Amalfi Coast. Cruise along the vertical cliffs, passing historic watchtowers and deep gorges. Drop anchor off Positano, the cliffside village of pastel-colored houses stacked above a black sand beach. Tender ashore to browse local linen shops and art galleries, or enjoy a final lunch on board surrounded by the dramatic vertical landscape. Cruise to the historic town of Amalfi for disembarkation, where your private chauffeur will be waiting to transport you to Naples Airport for your flight home.

Recommended Yacht Class & Specifications

Cruising the open waters from the French Riviera to Corsica and across the Tyrrhenian Sea requires a vessel with robust performance, stability, and open-ocean cruising range. We recommend a **40-meter to 50-meter (130ft to 160ft) displacement or semi-displacement motor yacht** or a large luxury catamaran.

Vessels of this scale offer crucial features for this route:

  • Zero-Speed Stabilizers: Essential for maintaining comfort at anchor, particularly in open bays like Calvi or Capri where swells can occur.
  • Cruising Range & Fuel Capacity: A hull with a range of at least 1,500 nautical miles ensures you can complete open-sea legs without mid-trip refueling delays.
  • Tender & Toy Capability: A large garage or deck space for a high-speed jet tender (6m+) and sea bobs is critical for exploring shallow caves (like Bonifacio or Capri's grottos).
  • Internal Space & Luxury: Accommodations for 10 to 12 guests in 5 or 6 en-suite staterooms, including a main-deck master suite, expansive salons, and a jacuzzi deck.

Best Time of Year for this Itinerary

The cruising season for Western Mediterranean itineraries runs from mid-May through October.

  • Peak Season (July & August): Offers guaranteed hot weather, lively shore-side energy, and peak glamorous atmosphere. However, marinas (particularly in Capri and Saint-Tropez) are heavily congested, and berths must be booked months in advance.
  • Shoulder Season (June & September): Highly recommended. June offers long daylight hours and beautiful flowers. September, known as the "connoisseur's month," features warm waters (having heated all summer), far less congestion in the anchorages, and slightly more flexible berth availability, while maintaining beautiful summer temperatures.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Monaco to Amalfi yacht charter cost?

Base weekly rates start at approximately $80,000 for a 30m yacht and range from $150,000 to $280,000+ for a 40m-50m superyacht. In addition to the base rate, you must pay the Advance Provisioning Allowance (APA, 30% to cover fuel, food, and dockage), local VAT (20% to 22%), and crew tips (15% customary). The total estimated weekly cost ranges from $240,000 to $430,000 for a recommended 40m-50m vessel.

What is the best season for a Monaco to Amalfi charter?

The optimal cruising window is between mid-June and September. July and August offer the hot weather and high-society scene but require booking berths months in advance. September is highly recommended by connoisseurs, as sea temperatures are warm, and the coastal towns of Amalfi and Capri are less congested.

What size and type of yacht is recommended for this route?

We recommend a 40m+ (130ft+) displacement or semi-displacement motor yacht. This type of vessel has the hull stability, zero-speed stabilizers, and open-ocean range necessary to cross the Ligurian and Tyrrhenian seas comfortably, while providing ample room for guests and water toys.

How many days are needed to cruise from Monaco to Amalfi?

A minimum of 7 days is required to cover the ~450 nautical miles, allowing for overnight crossings and daily island stops. However, a 10-day or 14-day charter is highly recommended to allow for a more relaxed pace with extended stays in Corsica, Sardinia's Costa Smeralda, and the Ponza islands.

What are the primary ports of call on this itinerary?

A classic route starts in Port Hercule (Monaco), stopping in Saint-Tropez or Cannes, Calvi and Bonifacio (Corsica), La Maddalena and Porto Cervo (Sardinia), the volcanic island of Ponza, Capri, and finally Positano and Amalfi.

Are VAT and crew gratuity included in the base rate?

No. The base charter rate covers only the rental of the yacht and the crew's base salary. The Advance Provisioning Allowance (APA, 30%), regional VAT (ranging from 20% to 22%), and customary crew tips (15% to 20%) are billed separately to cover your specific consumption and reward exceptional service.
