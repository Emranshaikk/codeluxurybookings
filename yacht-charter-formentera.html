The Formentera Specialty

Day Charters in Formentera: Bareboat vs. Skippered

Because Formentera is only 11 nautical miles from Ibiza and possesses some of the clearest turquoise shallows in Europe, more than 75% of visiting yachts are on day charters. Understanding the legal and operational differences is crucial before booking:

1. Day Charter — No License Required (Bareboat)

Under Spanish Royal Decree regulations, powerboats up to 5 meters in length with engines up to 15 hp (11 kW), as well as sailing boats up to 6 meters, can be skippered without any nautical license. Operating during daylight hours within 2 miles of port, these license-free craft allow independent exploration between Marina de La Savina, Cala Saona, and the protected edge of Ses Illetes.

2. Day Charter with Professional Skipper (Recommended)

For vessels larger than 5 meters (e.g. 38ft–50ft open day cruisers and catamarans), a professional skipper (adding approx. €90–€100/day) is essential. Your skipper takes legal responsibility for navigation, monitors Balearic Sea wind shifts, navigates strict Posidonia conservation zones, and coordinates restaurant zodiac tenders to bring you directly to your table at Beso Beach or Juan y Andrea.

3. Multi-Day & Weekly Bareboat Expeditions

Licensed skippers holding an International Certificate of Competence (ICC) or RYA Day Skipper can charter bareboat sailing monohulls and catamarans from 36ft to 55ft. Review our comprehensive Bareboat Charter Guide for license validation, transit log requirements, security deposit procedures, and seasonal weather windows.

The Posidonia Protection Rule

Formentera's blindingly turquoise water exists because of UNESCO-protected Posidonia oceanica seagrass meadows filtering the sea. Balearic environmental patrol boats actively inspect anchorages using underwater cameras and sonar.

Crucial Rule: Dropping an anchor or chain onto Posidonia meadows carries administrative fines ranging from €1,000 to €30,000. You may only drop anchor on pure white sand or hook onto pre-booked government eco-buoys in Ses Illetes and Espalmador.

Essential Formentera Ports & Anchorages

Though only 19 kilometers from end to end, Formentera contains some of the Mediterranean's most sought-after maritime anchorages. Here are the five key locations every charter guest must know:

Port of La Savina

The primary maritime gateway to Formentera. Home to Marina de Formentera and Marina de Poniente, La Savina accommodates yachts up to 38 meters with fuel berths, fresh water, provisioning services, and passenger tender docks. It is the handover base for all local license-free day rentals.

Main Departure & Fuel Hub

Platja de Ses Illetes

Consistently voted among the finest beaches on earth. A slender tongue of powdery white sand flanked by translucent turquoise waters. Anchoring is strictly regulated on sand or government buoys. Iconic dining spots like Beso Beach, Juan y Andrea, and El Pirata dispatch zodiac tenders straight to your yacht.

World-Class Beach Club Scene

Isla de S'Espalmador

An uninhabited, privately owned 2-square-kilometer natural reserve islet located just north of Formentera across the shallow Pas de Trucadors strait. Platja de S'Alga features a calm sheltered cove and pristine natural mud lagoon, making it a favorite morning swim stop on Ibiza-to-Formentera day charters.

Untouched Nature Reserve

Cala Saona

Enclosed by dramatic red sandstone cliffs on Formentera’s west coast, Cala Saona is a protected horseshoe bay with crystal-clear turquoise waters. It offers excellent paddleboarding along sea caves and is the island’s most celebrated anchorage for sunset cocktails before returning to Ibiza.

Sunset Cocktails & Snorkeling

Es Pujols & Platja Migjorn

Es Pujols provides an active beachside anchorage with easy tender access to boutique nightlife and dining. On the south coast, Platja de Migjorn stretches for 5 kilometers of wild dunes and bohemian beach shacks like Blue Bar, providing sheltered anchorages when strong northerly Tramuntana winds blow.

Sheltered South Coast Option

Ibiza & Mallorca Crossing Logistics

The Ibiza Crossing: At just 11 nautical miles, transiting between Marina Botafoch/Marina Ibiza and Ses Illetes takes 30 to 45 minutes by luxury motor yacht or 1.5 hours by catamaran. Most charter guests depart Ibiza at 10:30 AM, spend the day exploring Espalmador and Illetes, and return after sunset.

The Mallorca Crossing: For guests cruising from Palma, Port Andratx, or Puerto Portals, the offshore passage is approximately 70–80 nautical miles (approx. 2.5–3.5 hours on a performance motor yacht). Frequently Asked Questions: Formentera Charters

Specific, numeric guidance on licenses, charter pricing, routes, and seasonal conditions.

1. Do I need a license to charter a boat in Formentera?
No license is required for motorboats under 5 meters in length with engine power up to 15 hp (11.26 kW), or sailing boats under 6 meters without an engine, operated during daylight hours within 2 nautical miles of port. Anyone 18 years or older can operate these following a short safety briefing. For vessels exceeding these specifications, you must possess an accredited nautical license (such as an ICC, RYA Day Skipper, or Spanish PER) or hire a professional local skipper (available across our fleet for approx. €90 to €100 per day).
2. How much does a day charter in Formentera cost?
A license-free bareboat day rental starts between €270 and €480 per day depending on the month (May/October vs. July/August), plus fuel (€40–€70). A 35ft–45ft premium day cruiser or catamaran with a professional skipper, fuel, and towels averages €1,200 to €2,800 per day. Weekly crewed motor yachts range from €9,000 to €13,500/week, while sailing monohulls and catamarans rent from €2,800 to €7,500/week plus 21% Spanish VAT and fuel/APA.
3. What's the best way to combine Ibiza and Formentera in one trip?
The ideal itinerary is a private day yacht charter departing Ibiza (Marina Botafoch or Marina Ibiza) at 10:30 AM. The 11-nautical-mile crossing across the Freus passage takes just 30 to 45 minutes on a motor yacht. Anchor first at the uninhabited islet of S'Espalmador for swimming in the natural lagoon, cruise down to Platja de Ses Illetes for lunch at Juan y Andrea or Beso Beach (using their dedicated tender service), spend the afternoon anchored off the red cliffs of Cala Saona, and return to Ibiza at sunset.
4. When is the best time of year to charter in Formentera?
The Balearic charter season runs from May through October. July and August offer the highest temperatures (30°C–32°C), warmest waters (26°C–28°C), and peak beach club atmosphere, though anchorages are crowded and peak summer rates apply. For the optimal combination of warm sea temperatures, calm breezes, uncrowded anchorages, and 20% to 35% lower charter rates, June and September are widely recommended by our skippers.
5. Bareboat vs. skippered — which should I choose?
Choose bareboat if you hold an ICC or Day Skipper certification (or are taking a license-free <5m day boat) and want total privacy. Choose a skippered charter (selected by over 85% of our clientele) because Formentera strictly enforces Posidonia oceanica seagrass conservation with fines up to €30,000 for improper anchoring. A certified local skipper takes full legal responsibility, reserves government eco-buoys, guides you to sheltered coves depending on daily wind direction, and arranges tender transfers to beach clubs.
