The Economics of a Mykonos Yacht Charter

Chartering in Greece operates under the standard MYBA (Mediterranean Yacht Brokers Association) contract structure. The published charter price is the "Base Rate," which covers the vessel, the professional crew, and comprehensive marine insurance. Operating expenses, taxes, and crew recognition are calculated separately:

Transparent Cost Breakdown: Beyond the Base Fee

  • APA (Advance Provisioning Allowance): 30%–40% — Deposited approximately one month before embarkation. The captain uses this expense account for all fuel consumed by the yacht and water toys, guest food preferences, fine wines and champagnes, and island dockage fees. You receive a fully itemized ledger upon disembarkation; any leftover balance is refunded in cash or wire.
  • Greek Charter VAT: ~12% — In Greece, commercial charter contracts carry a statutory Value Added Tax of 12% (reduced to ~6.5% for specific commercial offshore licenses). This is charged on the base rate.
  • Crew Gratuity: 10%–20% — While at the charterer's discretion, international MYBA standards recommend a gratuity of 10% to 20% of the base charter fee to reward the captain, chef, deckhands, and stewardesses for exceptional around-the-clock service.

The Definitive 7-Day Cyclades Yacht Itinerary

While Mykonos offers world-famous energy, the true beauty of an Aegean charter lies in island-hopping through the Cyclades. Our recommended 7-day routing balances legendary island beach clubs with tranquil anchorages, engineered to minimize exposure to the northern Meltemi winds:

  • Day 1: Embarkation in Mykonos (Tourlos Marina or Ornos Bay)
    Board your yacht at Tourlos Marina or anchor in the sheltered amphitheater of Ornos Bay. After a welcome champagne toast and safety briefing from the captain, tender into Little Venice for sunset cocktails overlooking the iconic windmills before returning to the yacht for a starlit dinner prepared by your private chef. (Cruising: ~6 NM)
  • Day 2: Sacred Delos & Turquoise Rhenia
    Cruise 10 nautical miles southwest (~45 minutes) to Delos, the UNESCO World Heritage mythological birthplace of Apollo and Artemis. Explore ancient marble ruins on a private VIP guided archaeological tour. By afternoon, cruise minutes across to uninhabited Rhenia island. Drop anchor in South Rhenia's secluded turquoise lagoon for seabobbing, paddleboarding, and a sunset barbecue on pristine sands with zero crowds. (Cruising: ~10 NM)
  • Day 3: Paros & Chic Naoussa Harbor
    Set course south-southwest for Paros (~22 nautical miles, ~1 hour 15 minutes). Cruise past the dramatic granite rock formations of Kolymbithres Bay for an afternoon swim. As evening approaches, tender into the whitewashed Venetian fishing harbor of Naoussa for designer boutique shopping, vibrant cocktail bars, and Michelin-guide seafood dining right at the water's edge. (Cruising: ~22 NM)
  • Day 4: Antiparos & Despotiko Sea Caves
    A short cruise (~15 nautical miles, ~50 minutes) brings you to tranquil Antiparos, a discreet retreat for Hollywood royalty and tech founders. Explore the dramatic sea caves along the island's southern cliffs by tender or jet ski. Cross the narrow channel to the uninhabited sanctuary of Despotiko for absolute seclusion and a private beach lunch hosted by your crew. (Cruising: ~15 NM)
  • Day 5: Sifnos — The Gastronomy Capital of the Aegean
    Sail southwest toward Sifnos (~24 nautical miles, ~1 hour 20 minutes), celebrated as the culinary heart of Greece. Anchor in the horseshoe-shaped, protected bay of Vathi or Platis Gialos. Indulge in authentic Aegean clay-pot gastronomy, visit the clifftop Monastery of Chrissopigi, and savor artisanal Greek wine pairings while watching the sunset over the dramatic rocky headlands. (Cruising: ~24 NM)
  • Day 6: Milos & Kleftiko Pirate Hideouts
    Cruise west toward the volcanic wonder of Milos (~28 nautical miles, ~1 hour 30 minutes). Anchor among the towering white limestone sea arches and secret coves of Kleftiko—a former pirate hideaway accessible only by boat. Spend the afternoon snorkeling through luminous sea caves, exploring the lunar landscapes of Sarakiniko, and relaxing on your sundeck jacuzzi. (Cruising: ~28 NM)
  • Day 7: Syros & Return to Mykonos
    Navigate north-northeast to Syros (~42 nautical miles, ~2 hours), the aristocratic capital of the Cyclades. Admire the grand 19th-century neoclassical mansions and marble plazas of Ermoupoli. In the late afternoon, make your final crossing back to Mykonos for a celebratory farewell dinner at Scorpios or Spilia seaside restaurant. (Cruising: ~42 NM)

Choosing Your Vessel: Motor Yacht vs. Catamaran vs. Superyacht

Navigating the Cyclades requires matching your yacht's hull design and propulsion to your personal travel style:

Planing Motor Yachts (60ft–90ft): For travelers who prioritize speed and wish to cover long distances between Mykonos, Paros, and Santorini in under 90 minutes, planing motor yachts (such as the Sunseeker Predator 74 or Azimut 68) are unmatched. Their cruising speeds of 24 to 28 knots allow you to outrun weather changes and maximize time ashore.

Luxury Catamarans (60ft–80ft): For families seeking expansive lounging space, horizontal stability at anchor, and shallow draft to enter secluded coves, luxury sailing and power catamarans (such as the Sunreef 80) provide massive flybridges, lower fuel consumption, and supreme comfort.

Superyachts (100ft+ with Zero-Speed Stabilizers): In the Aegean, zero-speed gyro and fin stabilizers are indispensable. They eliminate roll both underway and when anchored in exposed bays, ensuring effortless sleep and five-star relaxation regardless of swell.

Frequently Asked Questions: Mykonos Yacht Charter

How much does a yacht charter cost in Mykonos?
A luxury yacht charter in Mykonos starts from approximately €25,000 to €55,000 per week for a boutique 60ft to 80ft motor yacht (e.g., Azimut 68 or Sunseeker Predator 74). Superyachts between 100ft and 140ft (e.g., Princess X95 or Benetti Classic 120) typically range from €85,000 to €160,000 per week, while 150ft+ megayachts exceed €250,000 per week. On top of the base charter fee, clients should budget 30% to 40% for the Advance Provisioning Allowance (APA), Greek charter VAT (typically 12%), and a customary 10% to 20% crew gratuity.
What is APA and how much should I budget for it?
APA stands for Advance Provisioning Allowance. It is the operating expense fund deposited before embarkation to cover fuel, gourmet food, fine wines and spirits, port and marina fees, and communications. For motor yachts cruising the Cyclades, APA is typically budgeted at 30% to 40% of the base charter rate (or 20% to 25% for sailing catamarans). The captain maintains an itemized ledger with receipts, and any unspent funds are refunded directly to you at the end of the charter.
Is VAT charged on yacht charters in Greece?
Yes. Value Added Tax (VAT) is legally required on commercial yacht charters embarking in Greek waters. The standard statutory VAT rate for charter contracts in Greece is 12% for vessels carrying commercial charter licenses. Certain qualifying commercial vessels sailing specific international or extended offshore routes may benefit from a reduced rate (approximately 6.5%), but clients should anticipate 12% in their total liquid budget.
What is the best month to charter in Mykonos to avoid the Meltemi winds?
The ideal months to charter in Mykonos are June and September. During these shoulder-season months, the Aegean sea temperatures are warm, restaurants and beach clubs are vibrant, and the northern Meltemi winds are far calmer. In peak July and August, the Meltemi can blow at Force 6 to 8 Beaufort (22 to 35 knots), which may restrict cruising between open island channels and require staying anchored along the sheltered southern bays of Mykonos (such as Ornos and Psarou) or sailing south toward Paros and Antiparos.
Crewed vs. bareboat: which is better for a first-time charter in Mykonos?
A fully crewed charter is strongly recommended for anyone chartering in Mykonos and the Cyclades. The Aegean Sea requires advanced seamanship due to sudden Meltemi wind gusts, strong tidal currents between Delos and Rhenia, and competitive stern-to Mediterranean mooring in island harbors. A professional captain manages all navigation, weather routing, and marina reservations, while a private chef and stewardess provide five-star hospitality.
How far in advance should I book a yacht charter in Mykonos for July or August?
For peak summer charter dates in July and August, you should ideally book 6 to 9 months in advance (between November and February). The most sought-after yachts—particularly modern builds equipped with zero-speed gyro stabilizers and low fuel burn—have their peak summer calendar booked well before spring.
